# MacBuddy

MacBuddy is a prototype social platform designed to help first-year McMaster University students form meaningful and lasting friendships. The project was created for McMaster’s INNOVATE 2Z03 course in response to the difficulty many students experience when trying to build close friendships after Welcome Week.

Rather than relying only on random social interactions, MacBuddy connects students based on shared interests, programs, residence, hobbies, academic goals, and the type of friendship they are looking for. The goal is to make meeting new people feel safer, less awkward, and more intentional. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## The Problem

Many first-year students meet people during orientation but still struggle to develop close and lasting friendships. Approaching strangers can feel uncomfortable, while existing social media platforms are not specifically designed to help McMaster students find compatible friends.

MacBuddy creates a structured and low-pressure way for students to connect with others who share similar interests, circumstances, and goals.

## Our Solution

MacBuddy matches verified McMaster students based on compatibility. Once two students connect, the platform supports them through each stage of building a friendship:

1. Students create a profile containing their program, residence, interests, hobbies, and friendship preferences.
2. MacBuddy recommends compatible students and explains what they have in common.
3. Matches receive icebreaker questions to make starting a conversation easier.
4. The platform suggests simple campus-based “side quests,” such as getting coffee, studying together, going to the gym, or attending an event.
5. Students complete activities, build a friendship streak, and earn progress toward badges.

These features are intended to help online connections develop into genuine in-person friendships. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Main Features

- McMaster student verification
- Compatibility-based friend matching
- Filters based on program, interests, residence, and friendship goals
- Guided icebreaker questions
- Suggested campus meetups and side quests
- Friendship streaks and progress tracking
- Achievement badges
- Profiles and direct messaging
- McMaster-inspired branding and interface

## Design and Development Process

Our team began by reviewing several individual solution sketches and identifying the strongest ideas from each concept. We selected the design that best combined compatibility matching, interest filters, side quests, progress tracking, and student verification.

We then developed a high-fidelity interactive prototype that demonstrates the core MacBuddy journey, including discovering compatible students, connecting with a match, completing an icebreaker, accepting a coffee side quest, and building a friendship streak. The prototype screens can be seen on page 10 of our second assignment. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## User Testing

We tested the MacBuddy prototype with five potential users. Overall, participants found the platform clean, easy to understand, and simple to navigate. The McMaster branding helped the concept feel relevant and trustworthy, and all five interviewees said they would be likely to use an app like MacBuddy, especially during their first year. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

The interviews also revealed several areas for improvement:

- Add profile pictures and longer biographies
- Include more specific interest and course filters
- Allow users to hide their activity status
- Add notifications for new matches
- Improve reporting, blocking, privacy, and safety features
- Test whether users actually complete suggested in-person meetups

Our next design iteration would focus on strengthening profiles and testing whether the suggested meetup feature successfully encourages students to interact in person. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
